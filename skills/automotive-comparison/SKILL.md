# Automotive Research & Comparison Skill

## Purpose
Produce exhaustive, variant-specific vehicle comparisons that are accurate, easy to understand, price-aware, and useful for a purchase decision. Never substitute a short feature summary or a collection of charts for the actual comparison.

## Mandatory comparison setup
1. Identify the main vehicle and exact variant. The main vehicle is the default retrofit target.
2. Select a competitor variant based on comparable on-road price, not automatically top-trim versus top-trim. A base, mid, or top variant may be selected if it is the fairest price match.
3. State model year, fuel, transmission, variant, ex-showroom price, estimated on-road price, source date, and price difference. Clearly label estimates and dealer-quote caveats.
4. Verify exact variant and model year from current official brochures, manufacturer pages, price lists, and credible sources. Do not transfer features from a higher trim to a lower trim.
5. If a fact is unavailable, write “Not published” or “Not verified”. Never invent specifications, prices, fitment, test results, or feature availability.
6. Cite sourced facts close to the relevant claims.

## Mandatory 14-category coverage
Every comparison MUST cover all 14 categories below. Do not stop after a few categories. Do not generate 14 charts while only writing 4 or 7 category comparisons.

For EACH category, include:
- A meaningful, sufficiently detailed comparison of BOTH exact variants.
- Relevant specifications, equipment, differences, and real-world implications.
- What is present, absent, basic, or premium on each variant.
- A category winner (or “Tie”) and a concise reason. If evidence is insufficient, say “No verified winner”.
- A /10 rating for each car, with a brief basis.
- Immediately after the category explanation and winner, insert that category’s pie chart. The chart must appear BEFORE the next category heading begins.

The 14 required categories:
1. Price, variant positioning & value
2. Engine & performance
3. Transmission, clutch & driveline
4. Fuel efficiency, range & running cost
5. Chassis, steering, handling & aerodynamics
6. Suspension, ride quality & ground clearance
7. Braking system & active safety
8. Passive safety, crash protection & ADAS
9. Exterior, body, lighting & visibility
10. Cockpit, dashboard, infotainment & audio
11. Seats, ergonomics & passenger comfort
12. Climate control, convenience & connectivity
13. Dimensions, cabin space, rear seat & boot practicality
14. Ownership, warranty, service, reliability, resale & total cost

### Detail expectations within the categories
Cover all applicable details, not merely the headline items:
- Powertrain: displacement, cylinders, aspiration, injection, power/torque and RPM, fuel compatibility, cooling, start-stop, drivetrain.
- Transmission: gear count/type, ratios if published, clutch, shift character, hill starts and overtaking suitability.
- Efficiency: claimed and real-world mileage separately, tank capacity, range estimate only when supported, service/running costs.
- Chassis: platform, steering type, turning radius, handling, stability, aerodynamic data (Cd etc. only if published).
- Suspension: front/rear architecture, tuning, dampers/springs if known, ride, body roll, load behaviour and ground clearance.
- Brakes/safety: front/rear disc or drum, ABS/EBD, ESC, hill assist, TPMS, airbags, ISOFIX, belts, ADAS functions, crash-test results and test protocol/year.
- Exterior: lamps, DRLs, fog lamps, grille, bumpers, sensors/cameras, wipers, washers, rain sensing, glass and mirrors.
- Cabin: materials, trim, steering, cluster, HUD, screen, audio hardware, phone connectivity, USB/12V, wireless charging, sunroof, storage and ambient lighting.
- Seats: driver/front/rear upholstery, manual/electric adjustment, ventilation/heating, lumbar/thigh/side support, headrests, armrest, rear split, legroom/headroom/shoulder room and floor hump. Do not invent foam density or ergonomic measurements.
- Climate/convenience: use the mandatory front-AC and rear-AC audit below; also cover auto-dimming IRVM, cruise control, keyless entry, push start, connected features and OTA.
- Practicality: all dimensions, wheelbase, boot, fuel tank, tyre sizes, spare wheel, loading access, rear wiper/defogger and storage.
- Ownership: warranty terms, service intervals/costs, service reach, parts, reliability evidence, resale and depreciation.

If a category has many sub-features, use subheadings or a compact table so the user can see the full audit. Do not collapse the category into one sentence.

### Mandatory front AC and rear AC comparison
Within Category 12, audit front and rear air-conditioning separately for BOTH exact variants. Never treat the mere presence of rear AC vents as proof of a separate rear AC system.

**Front AC audit — report each applicable item:**
1. AC control type: manual, automatic climate control, or FATC.
2. Single-zone or dual-zone climate control; state whether driver and front passenger can set separate temperatures.
3. Front cooling hardware and outlets; report compressor type/capacity only when officially published.
4. Front airflow direction and fan-speed controls.
5. Front cooling performance: use credible instrumented tests or clearly attributed owner/test evidence where available. Do not invent cabin pull-down time or vent temperature.
6. Front cabin filter: standard dust/pollen filter, PM2.5 filter, or other filtration; distinguish a filter from an air purifier.
7. Front passenger convenience: automatic climate mode, defog/defrost, memory/last-setting behaviour and controls, only where verified.

**Rear AC audit — report each applicable item:**
1. Rear AC vents: present or absent, exact location (rear console, B-pillar, roof, etc.).
2. Whether rear vents receive air from the shared front HVAC unit or use a separate rear HVAC unit.
3. Rear blower: separate blower present/absent/not verified. Do not assume rear vents have a dedicated blower.
4. Rear fan-speed control: separate control present/absent; identify its location and control levels if published.
5. Rear temperature control: independent temperature setting present/absent. Distinguish this from a simple vent-open/close dial.
6. Rear airflow direction and shut-off controls, if provided.
7. Rear cooling effectiveness: cite instrumented tests or attributed evidence; do not infer performance merely from vent presence.
8. Rear passenger reach, vent placement and likely airflow coverage; distinguish measured facts from reasoned observations.
9. Rear cabin filtration or air-purifier coverage, if explicitly specified; do not assume the front filter proves rear-specific filtration.
10. Clearly label each feature as standard, optional/accessory, absent, or not verified for the exact trim.

**Required comparison summary:**
- Include a front-AC comparison table and a separate rear-AC comparison table.
- State explicitly whether each vehicle has rear vents, a separate rear blower, independent rear fan-speed control and independent rear temperature control.
- Explain the practical difference for front passengers and rear passengers separately.
- Name a winner for front AC and rear AC independently, or state “No verified winner” if comparable evidence is insufficient.
- Do not call rear console vents “rear AC with independent control” unless the separate blower and control hardware are verified.
- Do not call a PM2.5 cabin filter an air purifier. Explain the distinction.
- If manufacturer brochures omit a detail, mark it “Not published” or “Not verified”; do not fill gaps from a higher trim, another market, or a different model year.

## Category chart placement and scoring
The order is mandatory:
1. Category heading.
2. Detailed comparison table/explanation.
3. Winner and reason.
4. Both cars' /10 scores.
5. That category's pie chart immediately below.
6. Only then begin the next category.

Never collect all 14 category pie charts in a separate gallery at the end. Never place a chart beside the category content if it interrupts reading. The pie chart must follow the relevant category's winner/rating and precede the next category.

### Rating colour convention
Use consistent colour semantics in category score displays:
- Main car scores higher: green.
- Main car scores lower: red.
- Equal scores: neutral grey.
Apply the same meaning consistently in badges or score labels. Do not use colour to imply an unverified fact. State clearly that scores are analytical estimates, not manufacturer or laboratory ratings.

### Pie chart rules
- Each category gets its own two-car pie chart immediately after that category.
- Pie slices represent the two cars' relative scores within that category only. Label this as “rating share”; never imply market share or probability.
- Display the numeric /10 scores next to the chart or in its legend because pie percentages alone are not intuitive.
- If both scores are equal, show the tie clearly and use neutral/equal treatment.
- Do not create a pie chart for a category whose scores are missing or not defensible. Mark the category “Not rated — insufficient verified evidence” and explain why.

## Final summary and mandatory final charts
After all 14 complete categories:
1. Provide a 14-category score summary table.
2. Provide an overall line chart with categories on the X-axis and score 0–10 on the Y-axis; one line per vehicle. Use readable short category labels and a clear legend.
3. Provide a final overall pie chart comparing the two cars' overall ratings. Label it as relative rating share, not market share.
4. Calculate the overall score using the same weighting for both vehicles. Default to equal weighting unless the user explicitly chooses weights. Explain the formula and show the resulting /10 ratings.
5. Provide separate verdicts for performance, comfort/family use, safety/technology, practicality, ownership and value.
6. Give a clear final recommendation tied to the user's stated priorities. Do not let a tiny numerical score difference hide meaningful trade-offs.

## Retrofit analysis
The main vehicle defines the retrofit direction. Identify useful features present on the competitor but missing/basic on the main car.
1. Check genuine OEM accessories/parts first.
2. Verify exact model/year/variant compatibility, part number, part cost, fitting, wiring/modules/coding, and warranty implications.
3. Only when OEM retrofit is unavailable, consider reputable aftermarket products and disclose safety/integration limits.
4. Mark each item: OEM Available, Aftermarket Available, Compatibility Check, Not Recommended, or Not Available.
5. Itemize parts + fitting + wiring/other components + installed total. Do not invent prices.
6. Do not treat aftermarket standalone alerts as equivalent to factory-integrated ADAS.
7. Do not include engine, gearbox, suspension, or brake mechanical conversions unless explicitly requested.
8. Calculate retrofit total and remaining price gap only when costs and direction are supported. Separate retrofit cost from resale value. Some vehicle-level advantages cannot be retrofitted.

## Completeness gate — do not finalize until passed
Before sending the final answer, verify:
- [ ] Exactly 14 category headings are present.
- [ ] Every category has a substantive comparison for BOTH selected variants.
- [ ] Every category includes feature/specification differences, winner or tie, reason, and two ratings (or a clear “not rated” explanation).
- [ ] Every rated category pie chart is directly after its own category and before the next category.
- [ ] No category is represented by a pie chart alone.
- [ ] Category 12 contains separate front-AC and rear-AC comparison tables for both exact trims.
- [ ] Rear vents, separate rear blower, rear fan-speed control and independent rear temperature control are each explicitly addressed for both vehicles.
- [ ] Front AC control type, zone configuration, filtration and verified cooling evidence are addressed.
- [ ] PM2.5 filtration is not mislabelled as a standalone air purifier.
- [ ] Final 14-category score table is present.
- [ ] Final overall line chart is present.
- [ ] Final overall pie chart is present.
- [ ] Overall /10 ratings and scoring method are explained.
- [ ] Unverified information is labelled; no higher-trim features have leaked into the selected variant.
- [ ] Retrofit section follows OEM-first policy where applicable.
If any check fails, continue the comparison and fill the missing category before declaring completion.

## Communication style
Use clear, accessible language. Explain technical terms briefly in plain language. Keep the comparison exhaustive but structured. Do not claim the audit is complete unless all 14 categories and the required charts are present.
